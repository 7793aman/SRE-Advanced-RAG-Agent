Incident Response Runbook

Internal SRE process document — not a Kubernetes upstream doc. Written for this project's fictional operations team, to match the incidents, alerts, and oncall_logs tables seeded for the Text2SQL demo.

Severity levels

  • P1 — Critical. Full or major service outage affecting production. Page on-call immediately; target response within 15 minutes.
  • P2 — High. Significant degradation, no full outage. Page on-call; target response within 1 hour.
  • P3 — Moderate. Limited impact, workaround available. Handle during business hours; target response within 1 business day.
  • P4 — Low. Cosmetic or informational. No paging; track in the backlog.

Standard remediation procedure

  1. Acknowledge the page and open an incident record (this becomes a row in the incidents table, with a start time and severity).
  2. Triage — check which cluster and workload the alerting pods belong to; run kubectl describe / kubectl logs on the affected pods to establish a working theory.
  3. Mitigate first, root-cause later — for a P1/P2, prefer a fast mitigation (rollback the deployment, scale up replicas, fail over to another cluster) over a full fix. Restoring service comes before understanding why it broke.
  4. Resolve — once the mitigation is confirmed working, mark the incident resolved. This sets resolved_at and, together with started_at, determines the incident's MTTR (mttr_minutes).
  5. Write the RCA — every P1 and P2 gets a short root-cause summary (rca_summary) within 2 business days: what broke, why, and what will prevent recurrence.

Reducing MTTR

The biggest lever on mean-time-to-resolution is fast, confident mitigation: rollback and scale-up should be one-command operations, and every production cluster should have a known-good previous deployment to roll back to. Alerts should link directly to the runbook page for that alert category so the on-call engineer doesn't have to guess at the first step.
